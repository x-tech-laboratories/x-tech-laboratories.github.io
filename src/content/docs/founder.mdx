Hi, my name is BeatLink. I'm an open source developer and the founder of X-Tech Laboratories. Right
now X-Tech is just me, building the apps I use every day. The goal is an ecosystem of them, the way
KDE and GNOME each have their own family of apps: one set of shared libraries, one design, one
architecture and one way for the apps to talk to each other and to everything else. Each app takes the
best ideas from the products that already exist, and AI is what lets a small team, or even one
person, build and maintain them all.

## The problem

Open source keeps solving the same problems in parallel, and users pay for it. In my years in the
community, I've watched the same pattern repeat:

- **Fragmentation.** Forks and competing projects each get one thing right and miss others, so users
  choose between trade-offs instead of a complete tool.
- **Thin maintainer teams.** A handful of volunteers carry large codebases, and burnout is common.
- **Slow progress.** With few hands and scattered effort, fixes and features land slowly, while
  proprietary competitors move fast.

The result is software that is free and trustworthy but often a step behind what it could be.

## What X-Tech is

X-Tech is an ecosystem of the everyday applications a person relies on: tasks, music, fitness, media,
budgets, backups, and in time the rest of what a desktop needs. There are only so many kinds of app,
and each one only has to be built well once.

Every X-Tech app is built on the same libraries ([X-Libs](/projects/#x-libs)) and the same design, and
follows the same [standards](/standards/), so a fix or a feature in one place reaches all of them. Each
one starts from a survey of what existing products do best, open source or not, and brings those
strengths together. It imports their data, and connects to them through plugins, APIs and open
standards where people keep using them.

I build X-Tech for myself first, so it keeps going whether or not anyone else joins. But it's free
software built in the open, and anyone who finds the apps useful is welcome to use them and help
shape them.

X-Tech is also meant to be a home for AI-assisted development. Many open source projects don't accept
AI-assisted contributions, and that's their right. But it leaves developers who use AI responsibly
with nowhere to build. X-Tech aims to be the place where they can create AI-friendly alternatives,
under clear rules for quality and transparency (see [How and why we use AI](#how-and-why-we-use-ai)).

I know a new project can easily become one more fork. The difference is consolidation. Instead of
splitting off to add one feature, each X-Tech app brings together the best of several products, with
plugins and adapters so nobody needs to fork it again.

## Principles and governance

Everyone has different tastes, so every X-Tech project will be built to bend rather than break,
following four principles:

- **Extensibility.** Core functionality can be extended without forking.
- **Interoperability.** Our apps work with each other and with the wider ecosystem, using open formats
  and standards.
- **Pluggability.** Features that not everyone wants live in plugins and adapters, not in the core.
- **Customisability.** We ship sane defaults for most people. Where opinions differ, there's a
  setting, a plugin or an adapter.

Common functionality moves into shared libraries, so a fix in one place improves every app that uses
it.

### Respecting the projects we learn from

Not every project X-Tech draws from will welcome this approach, and X-Tech doesn't need their approval
to build something new. I hold myself to these rules, and so will everyone who joins:

1. Follow every licence, and only combine code under compatible licences.
2. Credit the original authors clearly, in the code and in our docs.
3. Respect each project's AI policy. We never submit AI-assisted contributions to a project that
   doesn't accept them.
4. Contribute fixes and improvements back upstream wherever a project welcomes them.

### How decisions get made

For now, I make the decisions, but in the open, through public issues and discussions where anyone
can weigh in. As more people join, decisions will move to issues, discussions and polls. When
opinions differ, extra weight will go to two groups: long-standing members, and the people whose work
formed the basis of an X-Tech app or project, whenever they choose to take part.

## How and why we use AI

We use AI to enable rapid development, prototyping and maintenance. Its use is one of the most
controversial debates in open source right now, if not the most controversial, and for good reasons:

- **Consent.** Most AI models were trained on open source work without permission or consent from its
  authors.
- **Sustainability.** The data centres that run AI use massive amounts of water and energy. That
  affects our climate and burdens the local communities where they're built.
- **Corporate misuse.** For-profit corporations are developing and using AI in ways that don't always
  benefit their customers: replacing jobs, making platforms more addictive, creating fake personas to
  promote products, and influencing public opinion and rewriting online consensus.
- **Serious risks.** Mistrained, misprompted or maliciously used AI can enable widespread
  cyberattacks, election interference, or the development of bioweapons. That isn't a far-off
  dystopian future. It's a real risk today.

Here's my perspective. AI is a tool, and a very powerful one. But a tool has no inherent morality.
What matters is how it's used. A knife can be used to hurt someone, but it can also be used in
surgery to save a life.

And AI is here to stay. The AI bubble may pop, and companies may go bankrupt or consolidate, but the
technology itself isn't going anywhere. Malicious actors will keep using it maliciously, whether the
open source community accepts AI or sticks its head in the sand. So it's our job to use it for good,
in ways that are ethical, sustainable and empowering to everyone.

That's already happening. Individuals at home are building free and open source applications that
could, almost overnight, replace entrenched proprietary software that corporations have made billions
from (looking at you, Adobe). People are using AI to find and close security bugs that have sat in
foundational libraries and platforms for years. Others are using it to take back their privacy by
checking what closed-source software is really doing, and blowing the whistle on companies that
surveil and spy on their users.

AI is one of the most powerful tools and equalisers the open source community has for standing up to
exploitative for-profit corporations that only want their users' money and data. With AI, open
source projects can be developed faster, reviewed more quickly and maintained better, and they can
ship features that rival proprietary products.

But "it's just a tool" isn't enough on its own. Here's how I use it, and the rules X-Tech will hold
every contributor to:

| Concern | What we do |
| --- | --- |
| Code quality and reviewer burden | A human reviews and is responsible for every change. AI-assisted contributions meet the same bar as any other, and we don't accept low-effort generated PRs or bug reports. |
| Transparency | AI-assisted commits and PRs are disclosed: every AI-assisted commit carries an `Assisted-by:` trailer naming the tool, and every pull request says which parts AI wrote. See [disclosure](/guidelines/disclosure/). |
| Licensing and attribution | Generated code is checked for close matches to existing code, and we follow the original licence and credit when we find one. |
| Environmental cost | We prioritise local and distributed LLMs, and put development effort into improving them, so AI-assisted work can run on hardware people already own instead of in massive centralised data centres. We use AI where it saves real effort, not by default. |
| Who benefits | Everything we build is free and open source, so the value goes back to the community instead of to a platform. |

The only way any good comes from AI is if we band together and use it in ways that benefit everyone.
AI was built on the sweat, tears and work of the open source community. It's time we got paid for
that work.
